## Summary page (human-readable, not controlling text)

The ODbL summary says the license permits sharing, creating, and adapting a database subject to attribution, share-alike, and keeping-open conditions. It says public use of the database or works produced from it must be attributed in the specified manner; redistribution of the database or works must identify the license and preserve notices; publicly using an adapted database or works produced from one requires offering the adapted database under the ODbL; and redistribution with technical restrictions requires an unrestricted version as well. The page expressly says it is not the license and has no legal value; the full text controls.

## Full-license definitions

- **“Convey”** (Section 1.0): using the Database, a Derivative Database, or a Collective Database in a way that enables a person to make or receive copies. It expressly excludes interaction with a user through a computer network, or creating and using a Produced Work, where no copy of the Database or Derivative Database is transferred.
- **“Database”** (Section 1.0): a collection of material arranged systematically or methodically and individually accessible by electronic or other means, offered under the license.
- **“Derivative Database”** (Section 1.0): a database based upon the Database, including translation, adaptation, arrangement, modification, or another alteration of the Database or a Substantial part of its Contents. The definition expressly includes extracting or re-utilising the whole or a Substantial part of the Contents in a new database.
- **“Produced Work”** (Section 1.0): a work such as an image, audiovisual material, text, or sounds resulting from using the whole or a Substantial part of the Contents via a search or other query.
- **“Publicly”** (Section 1.0): to persons other than the user or persons under the user's control.
- **“Use”** (Section 1.0): acts restricted by copyright or Database Rights, including distributing, copying, publicly performing, publicly displaying, and preparing derivative works.

## Rights and conditions

- **Section 2.4** says individual Contents may have other rights and that the ODbL does not cover rights in individual Contents except Database Rights or contract.
- **Section 3.1** grants a worldwide, royalty-free, non-exclusive license to Use the Database for the duration of applicable rights, explicitly including commercial use. The examples include extraction/re-utilisation, creation of Derivative Databases, temporary or permanent reproductions, and distribution, communication, display, making available, or performance to the public.
- **Section 4.2** requires notices when a user Publicly Conveys the Database, a Derivative Database, or a Database as part of a Collective Database: use the license terms, include a copy or URI of the license, and keep copyright/Database Right/license notices intact.
- **Section 4.3** says creating and using a Produced Work does not require the Section 4.2 notice. If a Produced Work is Publicly Used, however, a notice must make users aware that Content was obtained from the Database, Derivative Database, or Collective Database and is available under the license. The example notice is: “Contains information from DATABASE NAME, which is made available here under the Open Database License (ODbL),” with links to the database and license where possible.
- **Section 4.4(a)** says a Derivative Database that is Publicly Used must be offered under the ODbL, a later similar license, or a compatible license.
- **Section 4.4(b)** says extracting or re-utilising the whole or a Substantial part of the Contents into a new database is a Derivative Database and must comply with Section 4.4.
- **Section 4.4(c)** says a Derivative Database is Publicly Used, and must comply with Section 4.4, if a Produced Work created from it is Publicly Used.
- **Section 4.5(b)** says using the Database, Derivative Database, or Collective Database to create a Produced Work does not itself create a Derivative Database for Section 4.4.

## Boundary on interpretation

These clauses describe the license's categories and conditions. They do not decide whether a particular Signalwatch normalization, live map display, short-lived cache, or stored observation set is a Derivative Database, a Produced Work, a Conveyance, or another category. That classification remains an **unresolved legal/terms question** requiring qualified advice and/or provider confirmation.
